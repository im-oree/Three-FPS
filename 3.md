## 1. Purpose & Scope of This Document

This document adds the **entire combat layer** to OPERATOR: a visible, animated weapon in the player's hands (the "viewmodel"), realistic firing/reload/ADS/switching behavior, per-weapon recoil patterns, hitscan damage detection, and the visual effects that sell every shot (muzzle flash, tracers, bullet impacts). This is the single most feel-critical document in the entire project — a Call of Duty–style game lives or dies on whether guns feel weighty, responsive, and satisfying, so every mechanic below is specified in detail rather than left to interpretation.

By the end of this document, a person must be able to click into the game, see a fully animated weapon in their hands that sways naturally as they move, fire it with correct recoil climb and per-weapon sound-ready event hooks, reload it with correctly-timed animations, aim down sights with a smooth FOV/spread transition, switch between at least three distinct weapons, and shoot static test targets that register hits with distance-based damage falloff — all inside the still-temporary Test Arena from Document 2.

**Explicitly out of scope for this document:** real levels/environments (Document 4 — this document adds a small number of static test-dummy hitboxes directly into the existing Test Arena purely to validate ballistics, not real level content), menus, HUD, and actual audio playback (Document 5 — this document must emit all the correct events at the correct times, but wiring those events to real sound files happens later), AI enemies and multiplayer (future, separate documents).

## 2. Foundational Definitions

**Viewmodel:** The weapon and arms mesh rendered attached to the camera, always visible in the foreground regardless of where the player's actual world-space body might be, animated independently from any third-person representation. In most FPS games this is deliberately rendered slightly "cheated" (its own depth/field-of-view handling) so it never visually clips into world geometry when the player stands close to a wall.

**Hitscan:** A method of resolving weapon fire where hit detection happens **instantly** via a mathematical ray cast from the firing origin along the aim direction, checking what it intersects, rather than simulating a physical bullet object traveling through the world over time. This is how the vast majority of modern shooter guns work (contrasted with slower "projectile" weapons like rockets or grenades, which are out of scope here but the system should not structurally prevent adding them later).

**Raycaster:** A Three.js utility (`THREE.Raycaster`) that, given an origin point and direction vector, computes which objects in a scene (and at what point/distance) that ray intersects — the core tool hitscan weapons are built on.

**ADS (Aim Down Sights):** The act of raising a weapon so its sights align with the camera's center, typically narrowing the field of view slightly (zooming in), tightening the weapon's accuracy/spread, and slowing player movement — the "aiming" mode as opposed to looser, faster "hip-fire."

**Recoil pattern:** A predefined sequence of camera pitch/yaw "kicks," one consumed per shot fired, that causes a weapon's aim point to climb and drift in a specific, learnable, weapon-signature way during sustained automatic fire — a core skill-expression mechanic in shooters, since players learn to counter-pull their mouse to compensate for a given weapon's pattern.

**Object pooling:** A performance technique where a fixed set of reusable objects (e.g., muzzle flash sprites, tracer line meshes) are created once upfront and then repeatedly reactivated/repositioned/reset on demand, instead of being newly created and destroyed for every single shot — critical for guns that fire many times per second without causing garbage-collection stutter.

**Skeletal animation:** Animating a 3D mesh by moving an underlying invisible "skeleton" of bones, with the visible mesh's vertices weighted to follow nearby bones — this is how weapon reloads, firing kicks, and character-style movement animations are authored and played back (via `THREE.AnimationMixer` in Three.js), as opposed to moving/rotating a rigid, unanimated mesh wholesale.

**Animation crossfading/blending:** Smoothly interpolating between two animation clips over a short transition window (e.g., 0.15 seconds) so switching from, say, an "idle" clip to a "fire" clip doesn't visually snap/pop, and so that two clips (like walking and aiming) can be combined simultaneously via "additive" blending rather than one replacing the other outright.

## 6. Weapon Data Model — `WeaponBase.js` + `/definitions/*.js`

### 6.1 Shared Behavior (`WeaponBase.js`)

A base class every weapon definition extends (or a factory function producing a consistent object shape — pick one pattern and apply it uniformly). Owns and manages:

- **Ammo state:** `currentMagazineAmmo`, `currentReserveAmmo`, with methods `consumeRound()`, `canFire()` (false if magazine empty or mid-reload/switch), `refillMagazine()`.
- **Fire timing:** given the weapon's `fireRateRPM` (rounds per minute), computes `secondsBetweenShots = 60 / fireRateRPM` internally, and exposes `canFireNow(currentTime)` gating based on last-fired timestamp.
- **Spread calculation:** `getCurrentSpreadAngle(movementState, isADS, isJumping)` — combines the weapon's own base hip-fire/ADS spread constants with modifiers based on the player's current movement state (queried from `PlayerController.getState()`/`isGrounded()`): e.g., spread increases while `SPRINT`/`SLIDE`/airborne, decreases while `CROUCH_IDLE`/stationary, decreases substantially while ADS. This single method is what `BallisticsSystem` (Section 13) queries on every shot to compute the actual random jitter applied to the raycast direction.
- **Damage falloff:** `getDamageAtDistance(distance)` — linearly (or curve-)interpolates between the weapon's near-range and far-range damage values across its defined falloff start/end distances, clamped at both ends.

### 6.2 Per-Weapon Definition Contract

Each file in `/definitions` exports a weapon config with **at minimum** the following fields (all consumed by `WeaponBase`, `RecoilSystem`, `ReloadSystem`, `WeaponViewmodel`, and later Document 5's audio/HUD wiring):

```js
{
  id: 'assault_rifle',
  displayName: 'Assault Rifle',
  modelPath: '/assets/models/weapons/assault_rifle.glb',
  muzzleSocketName: 'muzzle',

  damageNear: 35, damageFar: 20,
  damageFalloffStartDistance: 10, damageFalloffEndDistance: 40,

  fireRateRPM: 700,
  fireMode: 'auto',            // 'auto' | 'semi' | 'burst'
  burstCount: null, burstDelaySeconds: null,   // only relevant if fireMode === 'burst'

  magazineSize: 30, startingReserveAmmo: 90, maxReserveAmmo: 180,
  reloadTacticalDuration: 2.1, reloadEmptyDuration: 2.6,   // MUST match authored clip lengths

  adsZoomFOV: 55, adsInDuration: 0.22, adsOutDuration: 0.18, adsMoveSpeedMultiplier: 0.55,

  hipfireSpreadBaseDeg: 2.5, hipfireSpreadMovingDeg: 4.5, hipfireSpreadJumpingDeg: 7.0,
  adsSpreadBaseDeg: 0.4,

  recoilPatternId: 'assault_rifle',

  soundKeys: {
    fire: 'weapon_ar_fire', reloadTactical: 'weapon_ar_reload_tactical',
    reloadEmpty: 'weapon_ar_reload_empty', adsIn: 'weapon_ar_ads_in', adsOut: 'weapon_ar_ads_out',
    switchOut: 'weapon_generic_switch_out', switchIn: 'weapon_generic_switch_in',
    emptyClick: 'weapon_generic_empty_click'
  },

  hasTracer: true
}
```

`Pistol.js` and `SMG.js` follow the identical shape with their own tuned values (e.g., Pistol: `fireMode: 'semi'`, low mag size, high per-shot damage; SMG: very high fire rate, low per-shot damage, tight hip-fire but wider ADS spread than the AR — differentiate all three meaningfully, not just re-skins of the same numbers).

## 7. `WeaponManager.js` — Central Combat Orchestrator

- Holds at least two equipped weapon slots (`primary`, `secondary` — full loadout **selection UI** is Document 5's job, but the manager itself must support switching between two concrete weapon instances now; default to hardcoding `AssaultRifle` + `Pistol` equipped at boot for this document's purposes).
- Each frame (registered via `engine.registerUpdatable`), reads `InputManager.isActionDown()` for `fire`, `reload`, `ads`, `weaponSlot1`, `weaponSlot2` (new action names to add to `Constants.js`'s `DEFAULT_KEY_BINDINGS`, e.g. `fire: 'Mouse0'`, `ads: 'Mouse2'`, `reload: 'KeyR'`, `weaponSlot1: 'Digit1'`, `weaponSlot2: 'Digit2'`), plus mouse wheel delta for weapon cycling, and translates these into calls against the currently equipped weapon: `fire()`, `reload()`, `startADS()`/`stopADS()`, `switchTo(slotIndex)`.
- **Validates state before acting**: cannot fire while reloading or mid-switch; cannot reload if magazine is already full; cannot switch while already switching; starting a reload or switch while ADS active cleanly exits ADS first.
- Emits the following `EventBus` events at the precisely correct moments (these are the events Document 5's HUD/audio will bind to — this document's job is exact, reliable timing, not the consumption):
  - `weapon:fired` — `{ weaponId, remainingMagazineAmmo }`
  - `weapon:ammoChanged` — `{ weaponId, magazineAmmo, reserveAmmo }`
  - `weapon:reloadStart` — `{ weaponId, isTactical, duration }`
  - `weapon:reloadComplete` — `{ weaponId }`
  - `weapon:adsStart` / `weapon:adsStop` — `{ weaponId }`
  - `weapon:switchStart` — `{ fromWeaponId, toWeaponId, duration }`
  - `weapon:switchComplete` — `{ weaponId }`
  - `weapon:emptyFire` — `{ weaponId }` (attempted fire with empty magazine — dry-fire click hook for Document 5)

## 8. `WeaponViewmodel.js` — Rendering the Held Weapon

- On weapon equip/switch, loads the weapon's `.glb` via `AssetLoader.loadModel(weapon.modelPath)` and attaches the resulting mesh to a dedicated **viewmodel rig node** — a small `THREE.Object3D` parented to the main camera (so it inherits camera rotation automatically) but positioned at a fixed local offset representing "hand position" (e.g., slightly right and below screen center).
- **Prevents world-geometry clipping** using one of two acceptable standard techniques (choose one and implement it correctly): (a) render the viewmodel in a **separate render pass with its own narrow-FOV camera** sharing the main camera's rotation, rendered after clearing depth, so it's always drawn in front regardless of nearby walls, or (b) give the main camera a very small near-clip-plane specifically tuned so the viewmodel (kept intentionally very close to the camera) never intersects world geometry, combined with rendering the viewmodel on a distinct Three.js layer if extra isolation is needed. Document which technique was chosen with a code comment explaining why.
- Owns a `THREE.AnimationMixer` bound to the loaded weapon's skeleton, exposing `playClip(clipName, { loop = THREE.LoopOnce, crossfadeDuration = 0.15, clampWhenFinished = true })` — this is the method `AnimationStateMachine` (Section 12) calls into.
- On weapon switch, correctly disposes of (or hides/pools, if reusing loaded weapons repeatedly makes pooling more efficient — either is acceptable) the previous weapon's viewmodel object and mixer before attaching the new one.

## 9. `WeaponSway.js` — Procedural Movement Feel

- Tracks a smoothed/lagged history of recent mouse-look deltas (from `InputManager.getMouseDelta()`) and applies a small, spring-damped rotational offset to the viewmodel rig node, causing the gun to subtly "lag behind" quick mouse flicks and settle back to neutral — purely additive on top of whatever the animation system is doing, applied as a final transform tweak.
- Adds a slower, low-amplitude idle "breathing" sway (a gentle sinusoidal drift) when the player is stationary, for a sense of the weapon never being perfectly rigid/dead.
- Sway amplitude is significantly reduced (via a constant multiplier, e.g. `ADS_SWAY_MULTIPLIER = 0.25`) while ADS is active, reflecting a steadier, braced aiming stance.

## 10. Recoil System — `RecoilPatterns.js` + `RecoilSystem.js`

### 10.1 `RecoilPatterns.js` (Pure Data)

A data-only file mapping each `recoilPatternId` to an ordered array of `{ pitchDeg, yawDeg }` kick values, one entry "consumed" per shot fired during sustained fire (index resets when the player stops firing for longer than a short grace period, or clamps/holds at the pattern's final entry if fired well past the array's length — pick one behavior and document it; holding at the last value is the more standard, realistic choice for "climb plateaus" feel). Include a small randomized jitter range added on top of each step (e.g., ±10% of that step's magnitude) so the pattern isn't perfectly, robotically identical every single time, while still being fundamentally learnable/predictable (the deterministic base pattern dominates; jitter is a minor seasoning, not the majority of the movement).

Each of the three weapons must have a **meaningfully distinct** pattern (e.g., AR: steady upward climb with a slight rightward drift; SMG: faster, twitchier, more horizontal spread; Pistol: minimal per-shot kick appropriate to semi-auto fire).

### 10.2 `RecoilSystem.js` (Behavior)

- Subscribes to `weapon:fired` via `EventBus`.
- On each fire event, pulls the next pattern step for the currently equipped weapon and applies it as **two separate, coordinated effects**:
  1. An instantaneous camera pitch/yaw punch via a new, dedicated `PlayerCamera.applyRecoilKick(pitchDeg, yawDeg)` method (this document must add this method to `PlayerCamera.js` from Document 2 — distinct from the generic `shake()` method, because recoil needs a directional punch-then-spring-back-to-center behavior, not random tremor).
  2. A matching, visually exaggerated kick applied to the weapon viewmodel itself (a quick backward/upward jolt of the viewmodel rig, spring-damped back to neutral) — handled either inside `WeaponViewmodel.js` or `WeaponSway.js` (pick one and be consistent) as an additive offset stacked on top of normal sway.
- Recovery: after the player stops firing, the camera's accumulated recoil offset should smoothly spring/lerp back toward zero over a tunable duration (`RECOIL_RECOVERY_SPEED` constant) — this is what lets a player "ride out" a burst and settle back on target, standard shooter-genre feel.

## 11. `FireModeSystem.js`, `ReloadSystem.js`, `BallisticsSystem.js`

### 11.1 `FireModeSystem.js`
Encapsulates the pure timing/gating logic for `'auto'` (fires continuously while `fire` is held, gated only by `secondsBetweenShots`), `'semi'` (fires exactly once per discrete `fire` button press — edge-triggered, not level-triggered, must not fire again until the button is released and re-pressed), and `'burst'` (a single trigger pull fires `burstCount` rounds spaced `burstDelaySeconds` apart, then requires a fresh press to fire another burst). `WeaponManager.fire()` delegates the "should I actually fire right now" decision entirely to this module rather than reimplementing timing logic itself.

### 11.2 `ReloadSystem.js`
- Determines tactical (magazine partially full) vs. empty (magazine fully depleted) reload automatically based on current ammo state, selecting the correspondingly correct duration and animation clip name (`reload_tactical` vs `reload_empty`).
- Starts a timer matching the weapon definition's duration value (which must match the authored clip's actual length — call this out as a data-integrity requirement in a code comment).
- Updates `currentMagazineAmmo`/`currentReserveAmmo` at a sensible point relative to the reload's progress (e.g., roughly 60–70% through the duration, simulating "the new mag is now physically in") rather than only at the very end — implement via a simple scheduled callback keyed off elapsed-time-since-reload-started (a percentage-of-duration trigger), explicitly noting in a comment that this is a stand-in for true animation-embedded event callbacks, which could be adopted later if the asset pipeline supports exporting named clip events.
- Must be **cleanly cancelable**: if the player presses a weapon-switch action mid-reload, the reload immediately aborts (no ammo is granted, no completion event fires) and the switch proceeds normally.

### 11.3 `BallisticsSystem.js` — Hit Detection
- On every confirmed shot (post fire-mode-gating, post ammo-consumption), constructs a `THREE.Raycaster` from the **camera's true world position and forward direction** (queried via `SceneManager.getCamera()` — note: **not** the viewmodel's visual muzzle position, which is only used for spawning the muzzle-flash/tracer *visual* effects, a deliberate and standard distinction to keep hit registration always precisely centered on the crosshair regardless of viewmodel positioning quirks).
- Applies random angular jitter to the ray direction based on `WeaponBase.getCurrentSpreadAngle(...)` (Section 6.1) — converted from a cone half-angle into a small random pitch/yaw offset applied to the base forward direction.
- Casts against a **generic, registrable list of hittable objects** — expose `BallisticsSystem.registerHittable(object3D, metadata)` / `unregisterHittable(object3D)` methods. In this document, register: (a) the Test Arena's existing static collision geometry from Document 2 (for wall-impact effects, no damage), and (b) a small number of newly added **static test-dummy hitboxes** (Section 13) with a `takeDamage(amount)` method. This registration API must be architected generically enough that a future AI-enemy document can register enemy hitboxes into this exact same list with **zero modifications to this file** — state this explicitly in a code comment as a load-bearing extensibility contract.
- On a hit, computes damage via `weapon.getDamageAtDistance(distance)`, calls `.takeDamage(damage)` on the hit object if it has that method, and emits `EventBus.emit('combat:hit', { point, normal, distance, damage, surfaceType, isKill: false })` (the `isKill` field defaults to `false` and exists now purely so Document 5's UI and a future AI document don't need to add new payload shape later — just start setting it `true` where relevant) plus `EventBus.emit('combat:shotFired', { weaponId, hit: boolean })` for every shot regardless of whether it connected (used later for accuracy statistics).

## 12. Effects — `MuzzleFlashEffect.js`, `ImpactEffect.js`, `TracerEffect.js`

All three are implemented as small **object pools**: pre-allocate a fixed number (e.g., 20) of reusable Three.js objects at startup, and on trigger, grab an inactive one from the pool, reposition/reset its lifetime timer, and activate it — never `new` a fresh mesh/sprite/particle system per shot. Each pool's `update(dt)` (registered into the main loop) ages active instances and deactivates/returns them to the pool once their lifetime expires.

- **`MuzzleFlashEffect.js`**: a bright, very short-lived (2–4 frames), additively-blended sprite or small quad positioned at the weapon's `muzzle` socket world position (read from the viewmodel's socket bone transform), with slight randomized rotation and scale per activation for visual variety. Triggered on `weapon:fired`.
- **`ImpactEffect.js`**: triggered on `combat:hit`, spawns a small particle burst (a handful of pooled short-lived quads/sprites flying outward) plus an optional flat decal quad oriented to align with the surface normal at the hit point, positioned slightly offset from the surface to avoid z-fighting. Must read a `surfaceType` tag from the hit object's registered metadata (Section 11.3) to select a visually distinct variant — in this document, since only Test Arena walls (`"generic"`) and test dummies (`"dummy"`) exist, at least these two visual variants must be distinguishable (e.g., dust-puff for walls, a more organic-colored burst for dummies) — full surface variety (concrete/metal/wood) arrives with Document 4's real materials.
- **`TracerEffect.js`**: for weapons with `hasTracer: true`, spawns a thin, bright, fast-moving line/stretched-quad mesh traveling visually from the muzzle socket to the computed hit point (or out to max range if no hit), fading/shrinking quickly and returning to the pool — must feel closer to instantaneous+trailing rather than a slow visibly-traveling projectile, matching real tracer-round visual behavior.

## 14. Animation System

### 14.1 `AnimationBlender.js`
A thin wrapper around `THREE.AnimationMixer` providing: `crossfadeTo(clipName, duration)` (standard replace-with-blend transition for locomotion clips), and a distinct **additive layer** mechanism specifically for ADS — using Three.js's additive animation blending support (`THREE.AnimationUtils.makeClipAdditive` or manually blending a normalized aim-pose clip on top of whatever base locomotion clip is currently playing) so that, e.g., walking while aiming down sights shows correct simultaneous leg-walk-equivalent-sway plus raised-sight-alignment, rather than one animation completely overriding the other.

### 14.2 `/animation/states/*.js`
One small file per state, each exporting a simple object/class with a consistent shape (e.g., `{ getClipName(context), getBlendMode(), getCrossfadeDuration() }`) describing which clip(s) that state maps to and how it should blend in — e.g., `SlideState.js` maps to the `slide` clip with a fast crossfade; `ADSState.js` doesn't map to a standalone clip at all but instead tells `AnimationStateMachine` to engage the additive ADS layer on top of whatever the current base-locomotion state file resolves to.

### 14.3 `AnimationStateMachine.js`
Each frame, gathers: the current movement state from Document 2's `PlayerState`, plus this document's current weapon-action state (idle / firing / reloading / switching / ADS-active — tracked here as a small internal state derived from `WeaponManager`'s events, e.g., set to `FIRING` briefly on `weapon:fired`, `RELOADING` for the duration between `weapon:reloadStart`/`weapon:reloadComplete`, etc.), resolves both into the correct combination of base clip + optional additive layer using the `/states` strategy objects, and calls `WeaponViewmodel.playClip(...)` accordingly via `AnimationBlender`. This module is the single authoritative place where "what animation should be playing right now" is decided — no other file should call `playClip` directly.

**Critical integration requirement:** every movement-state transition established in Document 2 (walk↔sprint↔crouch↔slide↔jump/air↔landing) must correctly and smoothly trigger its corresponding animation clip **regardless of which of the three weapons is currently equipped** — verify this explicitly for all three weapons, not just the first one built.

## 15. Acceptance Criteria for Document 3

- [ ] All three weapons (Assault Rifle, Pistol, SMG) load correctly, are visibly distinct, and can be switched between via `1`/`2` keys and mouse wheel cycling, with correct switch-out/switch-in animation timing and correct firing-blocked-during-switch behavior.
- [ ] Firing produces a correctly-timed muzzle flash at the true muzzle socket position, a tracer (for tracer-enabled weapons) traveling to the correct hit point, and an impact effect correctly varying between wall and dummy hit types.
- [ ] Sustained automatic fire produces zero visible frame drops or increasing draw-call counts (verify via Document 1's `Debug.js` overlay) — proving effects are correctly pooled, not leaking/reallocating.
- [ ] Hitscan raycasts originate from true camera center (verify: hip-fire while strafing produces spread-correct scattered hits; ADS produces tight, near-pinpoint hits), and damage correctly falls off with distance against the three test dummies at varied ranges.
- [ ] Each weapon's recoil pattern is visibly, meaningfully distinct from the others, climbs predictably during sustained fire, and correctly springs back to center after releasing fire.
- [ ] ADS smoothly transitions FOV (via the modifier-stack API, correctly coexisting with Document 2's sprint FOV modifier without conflict), tightens spread, slows movement, and does not visually pop/snap on entry or exit.
- [ ] Reload correctly distinguishes tactical vs. empty based on real ammo state, times to each weapon's authored duration, updates ammo count at a sensible mid-reload point (not just instantly at press or only at the very end), and is cleanly cancelable via weapon switch with no ammo granted on cancel.
- [ ] Every animation clip listed in Section 5 plays and crossfades correctly across all movement-state × weapon-action combinations, for all three equipped weapons, with no visual popping or frozen/mismatched poses.
- [ ] Weapon sway (mouse-flick lag + idle breathing) is visibly present at hip-fire and correctly reduced during ADS.
